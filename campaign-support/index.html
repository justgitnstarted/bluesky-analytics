A petition starts the clock.

A petition creates immediate responsibilities and a short preparation window. Notices must be posted, information produced, and a vote held on a schedule the NLRB sets. Your counsel manages those obligations. We work the floor.

From the first conversation, we establish scope with counsel, confirm what consultants can and cannot say, and put people in front of your employees who can answer their questions directly. Employees get information. Leadership gets a clear picture of what is being said and asked.

What our education covers.

The subjects are set for each engagement with counsel and may include:

  • Employee rights under the National Labor Relations Act
  • The organizing and election process
  • The role of a bargaining representative
  • How collective bargaining works, and which outcomes depend on negotiation
  • Rights, responsibilities, and uncertainties on each side of the decision
  • Specific claims employees have heard, addressed with facts and sources

Our goal is informed understanding. Employees make their own decision.

The people your employees will actually talk to.

Our consulting network includes former union officials and organizers. They have sat on the other side of this exact conversation, and they know the questions employees ask and the expectations they bring to representation.

We match consultants to the workforce by background and language, including Spanish, Chinese, Tagalog, and Haitian Creole. A consultant employees recognize is a consultant they will talk to, and that is where accurate information starts to matter.

How we work with leadership and counsel.

Most clients come to us with labor counsel in place, and we work under their direction. Scope, responsibilities, and reporting are agreed in writing before anyone is on site.

For organizations without a dedicated labor team, we can bring affiliated attorneys who practice labor relations daily. Either way, the campaign runs with one legal strategy and one set of rules for everyone speaking with employees.

Our consultants' experience in employee representation also helps leadership understand the situation as employees see it and communicate responsibly through the campaign.

What leadership learns afterward.

Campaign education does not involve soliciting grievances or questioning employees about their support for a union.

When employees volunteer their experience of the workplace, recurring themes may be reported at the organizational level, under the engagement's reporting protocols, with attention to protecting who said what. Where the information supports it, the resulting recommendations help leadership understand the conditions behind the campaign and decide what to address next.

We do not deploy surveys once a campaign has begun. If you want to understand your workforce before a petition, see Proactive Assessment.
